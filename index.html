What's going on

Buying at a lower price does get you more shares. But investing a fixed amount every month already does that automatically, because €500 buys more shares when the price is low than when it's high.

The cost is in the waiting. While cash sits on the sidelines, the market usually keeps climbing, so when the dip finally arrives it's often at a higher price than where you could have bought earlier.

Historical data points the same way. In Even God Couldn't Beat Dollar-Cost Averaging, Nick Maggiulli tested a dip buyer who knew the exact bottom of every dip. Simply investing every month still came out ahead in most 40-year periods.

The price path is randomly generated, with about 8% average annual growth and 20% annual volatility. Illustration only, not investment advice.
